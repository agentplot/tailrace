## What is settled

- tailrace is one tool, organized around three concepts: artifacts, what
  was built; channels, what is released; environments, where it runs.
- It takes the place of a package manager's backend, Artifactory or GitHub
  Packages, with an OCI store: `push` after the merge queue, and consumers
  reading through `sync` or `serve`, everything signed and verifiable.
- Publishing to a package manager, to a Nix cache or to a live server are
  deployments to different targets of one system.
- Channels such as `beta` and `stable`, promotion between them, and
  environments tied to a channel under an `auto` or `manual` policy.
- Push and pull reconcilers are both supported; each target picks.
- The same store is moon's remote cache.
- The value is the three together: cache, artifact store and release
  channels over one content-addressed store.
- The family's tools keep their state in git.

## The direction

The design pack proposes these, and the statement takes them up or rules
otherwise:

- OCI as the substrate for everything, with npm, Nix and the rest as
  projections out of it, and the user's own registry as the default.
- Five namespaces drawn along trust and retention: cache, quarantine,
  artifacts, releases and config; attestations hang off their subjects as
  referrers rather than living in a namespace of their own.
- A thin center of identity, policy and a signed ledger; an edge machine
  writes only the cache and quarantine and never promotes.
- Keyless signing bound to an identity from the user's own provider; on a
  developer's machine the agent signs as the developer, delegated.
- A gate that promotes from quarantine to the artifacts by copying the
  same digest and writing a signed promotion record; an independent
  rebuild and two signatures before `stable` moves.
- Policies and environment definitions edited in git and packaged on
  landing; promotions as signed events, never edits.
- No reconciler for Kubernetes, where Flux and Argo consume the channel
  tags; reconcilers of tailrace's own where no good pull tool exists.
- Small things in git and large blobs in a file-based OCI layout synced to
  object storage, pointed at by digest.

## The invariants, as the statement will hold them

| | |
|---|---|
| I1 | An artifact is immutable and named by its digest; a version and a channel are pointers to one. |
| I2 | Nothing is rebuilt on its way to a release: the digest the merge queue signed is the digest a channel points at. |
| I3 | Every attestation is a signed statement attached to its subject's digest, and travels with it. |
| I4 | A channel moves only by a signed promotion, recorded append-only. |
| I5 | A consumer is served a version only when its attestations satisfy the policy. |
| I6 | The artifacts and the ledger stay in the user's account; tailrace keeps no copy. |

## What it leaves to others

tailrace lands nothing: sluice decides what lands, and tailrace takes the
landing as given. It builds nothing: moon and the repository's own tools
do, and tailrace packages what they left. It proves that bytes match
their source, never that the source is benign; bad source is caught at
the merge, by signed commits and review in sluice. And it runs nothing
itself: an environment's target does, once the reconciler hands it the
version.

## Questions the statement must answer

- Whether tailrace serves Nix store paths itself or layers on Attic.
- The policy language: Rego, CUE, Cedar, or in-toto layouts.
- The promotion record's schema, shared with any platform that keeps its
  own channels.
- Retention and collection per namespace, aware of Nix's reference graph
  where store paths live in OCI.
- The OCI conventions: artifact types, annotation keys, the tag scheme for
  channels and the versioning scheme.
- How moon's remote cache protocol maps onto the cache namespace.
- Whether a local-first store keeps its blobs in git LFS or in a file
  layout synced to object storage.
- Which agent reviews, what it signs, and how its identity is issued.
