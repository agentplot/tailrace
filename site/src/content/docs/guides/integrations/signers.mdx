## The contract

The seam is two operations (79, 80). **sign** takes a claim and the
identity of the party running tailrace and answers the claim signed, or
that no identity is at hand. **verify** takes an attestation and the
signers a policy names and answers which signer made it, or that none
of them did. A claim counts for a verdict only through a signer the
policy names and a claim that signer may make, never through what the
claim says of itself.

## Members

| member | profile | how | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| a key in AWS KMS | C.7, a key in a key service | a key the host's cloud identity may use and never export, through the host's role; the signer's name is the key's, and verification uses its public half | designed |
| a host key | C.8 | a key file on the host, as sluice's signers hold theirs; verification uses the public key the policy lists | designed |
| keyless with Sigstore | C.9 | a short-lived certificate bound to an identity from the owner's identity provider, logged in a transparency log, as Sigstore issues and cosign uses, public or private | designed |

The operator binds the signers for a host (92). A machine an agent runs
on holds an offering signer; the gate holds the accepting one; and no
host configuration holds both.

## Adding one

A signer profile implements the one signer interface and nothing of the
core. It passes the one conformance suite, which holds it to Parts A and
B; a profile that fails the suite is not one (93).
